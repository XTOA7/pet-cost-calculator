How the calculator works

Owning a pet is a long-term financial commitment. According to UK pet charity PDSA, the average cost of owning a dog over its lifetime can be over £30,000, with first-year costs often exceeding £2,000. This calculator uses average UK prices to give you a realistic estimate.

We calculate the "First Year" cost, which includes one-off expenses like adoption fees, neutering, beds, and initial vaccinations. We also calculate the ongoing "Monthly" cost, which includes food, insurance, flea/worming treatments, and vet checkups.

The 3 biggest hidden costs

  • Vet Bills: Even with insurance, you usually pay an excess. Routine dental care is rarely covered by insurance and can cost £300+.
  • Boarding / Sitting: If you go on holiday, kennels or pet sitters can easily cost £25–£40 per day.
  • Emergency Care: Out-of-hours emergency vets in the UK often charge £100 just to walk through the door, before any treatment.

Frequently asked questions

Is pet insurance really necessary?

Yes, in the UK it is highly recommended. Vet prices have risen sharply. A single emergency surgery (like swallowing a toy or chocolate) can cost £2,000 to £4,000. Insurance protects you from having to make decisions based on money during an emergency.

Why is a large dog so much more expensive?

Large dogs need more food, larger crates, bigger beds, and usually cost more to insure because they are prone to specific joint issues (like hip dysplasia). Many medications are also dosed by weight.

What is the cheapest pet to own in the UK?

Generally, small pets (like hamsters, rabbits, or small cats) are cheaper upfront and in food costs. However, all pets require vet care. If you want a dog, a small, healthy breed that doesn't require professional grooming will be the cheapest long-term option.
